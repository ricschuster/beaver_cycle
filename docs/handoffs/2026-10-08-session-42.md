## Completed

- #200, squash merged: a dam has a `level` (1 to 3, missing means 1). Costs are 8/4/2, 14/8/5, 22/12/9 (wood, mud,
  rock) and crests 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 m, up to `maxLevel` of the spot.
  - A whole dam that is good (condition 0.5 or more, not marked for repair) and below the spot's biggest level is
    raised by the next piece placed (`raised()` in `dam.ts`). It keeps what it has, and the pond rises from the old
    crest as it is built (`crestOf`). A worn dam is mended first.
  - The spot ring shows on a dam that can be raised. The HUD says "dam level 1 done, raise it to level 2: needs ...".
  - While being raised a dam does not wear, and it is new when the bigger level is whole.
  - A broken dam is built again from nothing at level 1.
  - The test menu "Finish dam here" fills level 1, and each press after raises and fills the next level.
- #201, open, not yet merged (these two commits missed #200, which merged before they were pushed):
  - The dam view lays the wall across the full width the water reaches at the dam line, in rows of 0.3 m up to
    the crest, mud along the foot and rocks on top (`damView.ts`). The author's catch: the old fixed 7 m pile did
    not cover the pond front, most at levels 2 and 3.
  - Cost follows the wall: table price times wall length at the level's crest over a typical one (`REF_WIDTH`
    16, 20, 22 m, the scan median), clamped 0.6 to 2 (`widthFactor`). Kept on the dam as `Dam.scale`. Raising never
    lowers it. Mending scales the same way (a wood unit mends 0.1 / scale). `settled()` now takes the spot.
  - The scan writes `dam_widths` per crest. A file without it prices every spot at 1.
  - Tests (304 unit, 10 dam-sites) and a "Cost follows the wall" note in doc 20.
